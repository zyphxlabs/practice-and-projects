## Incident Handler's Journal Entry

**Course:** Google Cybersecurity Certificate - Course 6 Sound the Alarm Detection and Response

This is my first entry in the incident handlers journal and its based on a ransomware scenario at a small US health care clinic. The point of this activity was to practice documenting a security incident the way an actual incident handler would using the 5 Ws format

### Date And Entry

Date July 23 2024
Entry number 1

### Description

Documenting a cybersecurity incident that hit a small health care clinic on a Tuesday morning

### Tools Used

None for this entry since it was more about practicing documentation structure than using an actual tool

### The 5 Ws

Who caused the incident was an organized group of unethical hackers who are known for targeting healthcare and transportation companies specifically

What happened was a ransomware attack where the attackers encrypted the clinics critical files and left behind a ransom note demanding a large sum of money for the decryption key

When this occurred was Tuesday at approximately 9 00 am and that timing mattered a lot because it meant business operations were disrupted right at the start of the work day

Where this happened was at a small US health care clinic that provides primary care services

Why this happened comes down to a phishing email that was sent to several employees and it contained a malicious attachment once someone downloaded it that installed malware on their machine and gave the attackers the access they needed to later deploy the ransomware

### Additional Notes

After going through the scenario a couple questions stuck with me

- How could this clinic prevent something like this from happening again since phishing is such a common entry point
- Should the company even pay the ransom or is that just encouraging more attacks down the line

That was when I realized how much damage a single phishing email can cause if employees arent trained to spot it. One person clicking one attachment and the whole clinic lost access to patient records and had to shut down operations completely
